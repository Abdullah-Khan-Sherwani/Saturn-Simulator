Technical Expectations
Baseline Requirements: All projects are expected to implement the fundamental techniques covered in lectures, adapted as appropriate to the chosen direction. These include the Phong reflection model with ambient, diffuse, and specular components; along with appropriate material properties such as shininess and reflectance coefficients. Lighting should preferably he computed per-fragment for improved visual quality. Projects should also support texture mapping; including diffuse maps and specular maps with proper sampling in the fragment shader: The system should incorporate multiple light types: directional lights, point lights with distance attenuation, and spotlights with smooth falloff using inner and outer cutoff angles. Correct accumulation of contributions from multiple lights is required. From an implementation standpoint, projects should demonstrate proper use of GLSL shaders, ineluding uniform management for lights and materials, and correct handling of transformations and surface normals.
Note on applicability: Not  every baseline technique will fit  every project   direction: If a technique does not apply to your chosen direction, state this clearly in YOur proposal and confirm with the instructor before proceeding:
Advanced Techniques: In addition to the baseline requirements, each project must implement at least two advanced techniques from the list below. The combined effort score of the chosen techniques must be at least 6 out of 10. Vote that selecting two low-effort techniques (e.g: = Fog Gamma Correction, combined score 4) does not meet this requirement; at least one technique must have an effort score of 3 or higher.
Shadow Mapping (Effort: 4/5) Renders the scene from the light's point of view to generate a depth map, which is then used during rendering to determine shadowed regions. Requires handling light-space transformations, depth comparisons, and resolving artifacts such as shadow acne_
Normal Mapping (Effort: 3/5) Enhances surface detail without increasing geometric Com- plexity by perturbing surface normals using a texture. Requires working in tangent space and constructing a TBN (tangent, bitangent; normal) basis.
Parallax Mapping (Effort: 4/5) Simulates depth within textures by adjusting texture coordi- nates based on the view direction and a height map. Provides a stronger illusion of depth than normal mapping but is more complex to implement.
Environment Mapping (Effort: 3/5) Simulates reflections by sampling from a cube map rep resenting the surroundling environment. Requires computing reflection vectors in the fragment shader.

Refraction (Effort: 3/5) Simulates the bending of light through transparent materials such as glass. Involves computing refraction vectors and applying an index of refraction to control the effeet .
Fog (Effort: 2/5) Enhances depth perception by bleudling object colors with a fog color based on camera distance. Linear O1 exponential fog models may be used.
Bloom (Effort:  4/5) post-processing effect that creates a glow around bright areas of the image. Typically requires extracting bright regions, applying a blur; and combining the result with the original image using framebuffer operations:
Gamma Correction (Effort:  2/5) Ensures that rendered colors appear correctly on display devices by converting from linear color space to gamma space.
Screen Space Ambient Occlusion (Effort:  5/5) Approximates ambient occlusion in screen space by sampling nearby geometry using depth and normal information. Significantly improves visual realism but is computationally and conceptually challenging:
Physically-Based Rendering (Effort: 4/5) Replaces empirical lighting models with physically motivated formulations using parameters such as metallic and roughness values. Produces more realistic and consistent results across different lighting conditions.
Deferred Shading (Effort: 5/5) Separates   geometry processing and lighting into distinct passes by storing intermediate data (position, normal, color) in a G-buffer. Enables efficient rendering with many light sources but requires significant architectural changes to the rendering pipeline. Due to its scope, this technique is best suited as the central focus of the project rather than a secondary addition.
Toon Shading (Effort: 3/5) Produces a stylized, cartoon-like appearance by quantizing light- ing values into discrete levels. May be combined with edge detection for a stronger visual effect.
